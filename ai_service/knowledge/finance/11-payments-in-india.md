# Ways to pay and transfer money in India

Limits and timings below are the usual ones; banks can set their own lower limits, and the rules change from time to time. Check your bank's app for the current figures.

## UPI (Unified Payments Interface)

- Instant transfers from your bank account using a **UPI ID** (like name@bank), a phone number or a QR code.
- Works all day, every day, including holidays.
- Run by NPCI. The usual limit is up to ₹1 lakh per transaction, with higher limits for some kinds of payments.
- A UPI payment is a **debit** from your bank account (money out); money received by UPI is a **credit**.

## IMPS (Immediate Payment Service)

- Instant bank-to-bank transfer, available 24x7.
- Uses the account number and IFSC, or a mobile number and MMID.
- Usual limit up to ₹5 lakh per transaction.

## NEFT (National Electronic Funds Transfer)

- Bank transfer settled in batches through the day; available 24x7 since December 2019.
- No minimum amount. Good for regular transfers that do not need to be instant.

## RTGS (Real Time Gross Settlement)

- For large transfers, settled one by one in real time; available 24x7 since December 2020.
- Minimum amount ₹2 lakh.

## UPI, IMPS, NEFT or RTGS?

| Need | Usually used |
|---|---|
| Small, everyday payment to a person or shop | UPI |
| Instant transfer by account number | IMPS |
| Regular transfer, no hurry | NEFT |
| ₹2 lakh or more | RTGS (or NEFT) |

## Cards and net banking

- **Debit card:** pays from your own bank balance immediately.
- **Credit card:** pays with borrowed money up to your credit limit; you repay when the bill is due.
- **Net banking:** paying or transferring through your bank's website or app.

## Automatic payments

- **Auto-debit / standing instruction / mandate (for example UPI AutoPay or NACH):** you allow a company or your bank to take a fixed or capped amount on a schedule, such as an EMI, SIP, insurance premium or subscription.
- Keep a list of active mandates and cancel the ones you no longer use.

## Staying safe

- Never share an OTP, UPI PIN, card PIN or CVV with anyone, including someone claiming to be from your bank.
- You need your UPI PIN only to **send** money, never to **receive** it.
- Report unauthorised transactions to your bank at once.

## In PPA

PPA does not move money. You record payments yourself: a UPI or card payment you made is an **expense**, and money you received is **income**. Moving money between your own accounts is a transfer, not spending.
