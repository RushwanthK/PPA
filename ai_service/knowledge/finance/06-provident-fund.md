# Provident funds (EPF and PPF)

Provident funds are long-term, government-backed savings schemes in India, mainly meant for retirement.

## EPF (Employees' Provident Fund)

- For salaried employees of organisations covered by the EPF scheme.
- A share of your basic salary is deducted every month, and your employer also contributes.
- It earns interest declared each year by the EPFO.
- Each employee has a **UAN** (Universal Account Number) that stays the same when changing jobs. Transferring the account to the new employer keeps the history together.
- Withdrawal is mainly at retirement or after leaving employment. Partial withdrawals are allowed for specific purposes, such as housing, medical needs, education or marriage, under the scheme's rules.

## PPF (Public Provident Fund)

- Open to individuals, including the self-employed, through banks and post offices.
- Long lock-in: the account runs for 15 years and can be extended in blocks.
- There are yearly minimum and maximum contribution limits.
- The interest rate is set by the government and reviewed periodically.
- It is popular for its safety and its tax treatment under Indian tax law.

## Why they matter

- **Forced discipline:** money is hard to withdraw early, which protects retirement savings.
- **Safety:** government-backed, low risk.
- **Trade-off:** low liquidity. This money is not an emergency fund.

Rates, limits and tax rules change. Always check the current details on the EPFO website or with your bank or post office.

## In PPA

Record your EPF or PPF balance as an **asset** with the category **Provident Fund**. Updating it once or twice a year (for example from your EPF passbook) keeps your net worth accurate.
