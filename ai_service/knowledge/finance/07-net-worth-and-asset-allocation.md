# Net worth, asset allocation and diversification

## Net worth

**Net worth = everything you own − everything you owe.**

- Own: bank and savings balances, investments, provident fund, property.
- Owe: credit card balances, personal loans, home or vehicle loans.

Net worth is a better measure of financial health than income. A high earner with large debts can have a lower net worth than a moderate earner who saves consistently. Track it monthly or quarterly. The trend matters more than any single value.

## Asset allocation

Asset allocation means how your investments are split between asset classes:

- **Equity** (stocks, equity funds, ETFs): highest long-term growth potential, highest short-term ups and downs.
- **Debt** (FDs, bonds, debt funds, provident fund): steadier, lower growth.
- **Cash** (savings): safest and most liquid, lowest return, and loses value to inflation over time.
- **Others** (gold, real estate).

Asset allocation usually has a bigger effect on long-term results and risk than picking individual stocks. A suitable mix depends on your goals, your time horizon, and how much fall in value you can tolerate without panicking.

## Diversification

Diversification means not depending on any single investment. It works on several levels:

- across asset classes (equity, debt, cash);
- within an asset class (many companies, not one);
- across sectors and, for some investors, countries.

It does not guarantee profit or prevent all loss, but it reduces the damage when one investment does badly.

## Rebalancing

Over time, the investments that grew fastest take up a larger share than you planned. **Rebalancing** brings the mix back to the target, for example once a year, by:

- moving some money from what grew to what fell behind, or
- directing new investments to the underweight part.

This keeps risk at the level you chose and builds in a habit of "selling high and buying low".
