# How credit cards and credit scores work

## The billing cycle

A credit card works in repeating **billing cycles**, usually about one month long.

1. During the cycle, every purchase is added to your balance.
2. At the end of the cycle the bank generates a **statement** (the bill) with the **total amount due** and a **minimum amount due**.
3. You then have a period until the **payment due date**, often around two to three weeks. If you pay the full total by then, you usually pay **no interest** on those purchases. This is the interest-free period.

## Minimum due: a trap

Paying only the minimum amount due avoids a late fee, but the unpaid part carries **interest**. Card interest rates are much higher than most loans, often more than 30% a year. Interest can also start applying to new purchases. Paying only the minimum is one of the most expensive ways to borrow.

**Rule of thumb:** pay the **total amount due** every month. If you cannot, treat the card balance as high-priority debt.

## Fees to know

- **Late payment fee:** if not even the minimum is paid by the due date.
- **Cash withdrawal (cash advance):** fees plus interest from the day of withdrawal, with no interest-free period. Avoid it.
- **Over-limit and foreign-currency charges:** depending on the card.

## Credit score

A credit score (in India, from credit bureaus such as CIBIL, Experian, Equifax and CRIF High Mark) summarises how reliably you repay credit. Lenders use it to decide on loans and cards and their interest rates. Main factors:

| Factor | What helps |
|---|---|
| Payment history | Paying every EMI and card bill on time. This matters most. |
| Credit utilisation | Using a small share of your card limits. Many guides suggest staying below about 30%. |
| Length of credit history | Older accounts kept in good standing |
| Credit mix and new credit | Not applying for many loans or cards in a short time |

**Credit utilisation** = amount owed on cards ÷ total card limits. For example, ₹30,000 owed on a ₹1,00,000 total limit is 30% utilisation.

## Using cards well

- Treat the card as a payment method, not extra income. Only spend what is already in your budget.
- Set up auto-pay for the full statement amount if possible.
- Check every statement for errors or transactions you did not make, and report them to the bank quickly.

## In PPA

The credit card page shows **unbilled spends** (current cycle), **billed unpaid** (what is due now) and **available limit**. Used ÷ limit is your utilisation for that card.
