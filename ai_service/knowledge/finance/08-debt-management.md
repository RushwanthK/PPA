# Managing and paying off debt

## Good and bad debt

Not all debt is equally harmful.

- Debt for something that keeps or grows its value, or raises your income, at a reasonable rate (such as a home loan or education loan) can be sensible if the EMI is affordable.
- High-interest debt used for day-to-day spending, especially **unpaid credit card balances** and some personal loans, is the most damaging.

## How much EMI is too much

A common guideline is that **all EMIs together should stay below roughly 30 to 40% of take-home pay**. Above that, any disruption to income quickly becomes a crisis. Lenders also look at this ratio.

## Two methods to pay off several debts

Always pay at least the minimum on every debt to avoid fees and damage to your credit score. Put any extra money towards one debt at a time:

| Method | Extra money goes to | Advantage |
|---|---|---|
| **Avalanche** | The debt with the **highest interest rate** | Saves the most interest. Mathematically best |
| **Snowball** | The debt with the **smallest balance** | Quick wins that keep you motivated |

When one debt is cleared, move its payment to the next one. Both methods work if you stick with them.

## Practical steps

1. List every debt with its balance, interest rate and minimum payment.
2. Stop adding to the highest-cost debt, for example by not using the card until it is cleared.
3. Keep a small emergency buffer so that a surprise does not create new debt.
4. Consider whether a lower-interest option, such as a balance-transfer offer or a loan against an FD, could replace expensive card debt. Read all fees carefully.
5. If you are struggling, talk to your lender early. Ignoring missed payments makes the options worse.

## Warning signs

- Using one card to pay another.
- Only ever paying the minimum due.
- Taking new loans to pay EMIs.

These mean the debt is growing faster than it is being repaid.

## In PPA

The credit card page and the dashboard's **credit card debt** figure show what you owe. Watching that number go down month by month is a good measure of progress.
