# Emergency fund

An emergency fund is money kept aside **only** for unexpected events: job loss, a medical bill, an urgent home or vehicle repair, or supporting family in a crisis. It is the foundation of a financial plan. Without it, every surprise becomes new debt, often expensive credit card debt.

## How much

A common guideline is **3 to 6 months of essential expenses**: rent, food, utilities, EMIs, insurance and school fees. Count essential expenses, not total income.

Aim for the higher end if:

- your income is irregular (freelance, commission or business income);
- you are the only earner in the family;
- you have dependants or ongoing medical needs.

## Where to keep it

An emergency fund must be **safe** and **quickly available**. Earning a high return is not its job.

Suitable places:

- a savings bank account, possibly a separate one so it is not spent by accident;
- a sweep-in or fixed deposit that can be broken early. A small penalty may apply.

Not suitable: stocks or equity funds. Their value can fall exactly when the emergency happens, for example during a downturn when jobs are also at risk.

## Building it

1. Start with a small target, such as one month of expenses.
2. Set up an automatic monthly transfer on payday.
3. Put windfalls such as a bonus, tax refund or gifts into it until it is full.
4. After using it, refill it before resuming other goals.

## In PPA

Create a dedicated **savings account** named, for example, "Emergency fund", linked to your main bank. Deposits move money out of the bank, so you can see the fund grow separately from day-to-day money.

*This note is general education, not personal financial advice.*
