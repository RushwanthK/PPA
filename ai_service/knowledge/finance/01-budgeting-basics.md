# Budgeting basics

A budget is a plan for where your money will go before you spend it. Its purpose is not to stop spending. It makes sure that spending, saving and paying off debt happen on purpose rather than by accident.

## Step 1: Know your income

Use your **take-home** income: the amount that actually reaches your bank account after tax and deductions. Planning with your gross salary makes every budget look better than it is.

## Step 2: Track where money goes

For one to three months, record every expense with a category such as rent, groceries, transport, eating out or subscriptions. Most people are surprised by small, frequent expenses. Tracking comes before cutting, because you cannot fix what you cannot see.

In PPA, adding a category to each bank and credit card expense builds this picture automatically in the dashboard's spending chart.

## Step 3: Split spending into needs, wants and savings

A common starting rule is **50/30/20**:

| Share of take-home pay | For |
|---|---|
| About 50% | **Needs**: rent, groceries, utilities, loan EMIs, insurance |
| About 30% | **Wants**: eating out, entertainment, shopping, travel |
| About 20% | **Savings and debt repayment**: emergency fund, investments, extra loan payments |

These numbers are a guide, not a law. In a high-rent city, needs may take more than half. The idea is to decide the split consciously.

## Step 4: Pay yourself first

Move your savings amount out **at the start of the month**, as soon as income arrives, for example into a savings account or an automatic investment. Saving "whatever is left at the end of the month" usually means saving nothing.

## Step 5: Review monthly

Compare the plan with what actually happened. Adjust the categories that are always over budget, either by spending less or by planning more realistically.

## Common mistakes

- Forgetting yearly or irregular costs, such as insurance premiums, festivals or annual subscriptions. Divide them by 12 and set that amount aside each month.
- Treating credit card spending as free until the bill arrives. Card spending is spending on the day it happens.
- Making the budget so strict that it is abandoned after two weeks.

*This note is general education, not personal financial advice.*
