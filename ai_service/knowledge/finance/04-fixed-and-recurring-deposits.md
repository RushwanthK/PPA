# Fixed deposits (FD) and recurring deposits (RD)

## Fixed deposit (FD)

You deposit a **lump sum once** for a fixed period (tenure), from a few days to several years, at an interest rate fixed when you open it.

- The return is predictable. You know the maturity amount in advance.
- Interest can be paid out regularly, or added back to the deposit (**cumulative**), where it compounds.
- Breaking it before maturity is usually allowed, with a penalty that reduces the interest.
- Senior citizens often get a slightly higher rate.

## Recurring deposit (RD)

You deposit a **fixed amount every month** for a chosen tenure. At maturity you receive all instalments plus interest.

- It suits people who want to save a regular part of their salary but do not have a lump sum.
- It builds saving discipline. Missing instalments can lead to penalties.

## FD vs RD at a glance

| | FD | RD |
|---|---|---|
| How you invest | One lump sum | Fixed monthly instalments |
| Good for | Money you already have | Building savings from income |
| Interest | Fixed at opening | Fixed at opening |
| Typical use | Parking a bonus, short-term goals, part of an emergency fund | Saving towards a goal over 1 to 3 years |

## Safety and tax

- Bank deposits in India are insured by the **DICGC** up to a limit per depositor per bank. The limit has been ₹5 lakh, covering principal and interest together. Check the current limit on dicgc.org.in.
- Interest from FDs and RDs is **taxable** at your income-tax slab rate, and the bank may deduct TDS. A high nominal rate can look smaller after tax and inflation.

## Where they fit

FDs and RDs are low-risk and predictable, which makes them good for short-term goals and safety money. Over long periods their after-tax return can be close to inflation, so long-term goals usually also need growth assets (see "Mutual funds, ETFs and stocks").

## In PPA

Record an FD as an **asset** with the category **FD**, so it appears in your asset allocation.
