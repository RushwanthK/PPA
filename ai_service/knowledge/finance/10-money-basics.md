# Money basics: debit and credit, income and expense

## Debit and credit in a bank account

On a bank statement, **debit** and **credit** describe which way money moved:

- **Debit (Dr):** money **going out** of your account. Paying a bill, buying something with your debit card, withdrawing cash or sending money by UPI are debits. A debit **reduces** your balance.
- **Credit (Cr):** money **coming into** your account. Your salary, a refund, interest from the bank or money a friend sends you are credits. A credit **increases** your balance.

Example: your balance is ₹10,000. You pay ₹1,500 for groceries (a debit), then receive ₹40,000 salary (a credit). Your balance becomes ₹48,500.

## Debit card and credit card

- A **debit card** spends **your own money**: the amount is taken from your bank account straight away.
- A **credit card** spends the **bank's money** up to your **credit limit**. You repay it later, when the bill (statement) is due. If you pay the full bill by the due date, you usually pay no interest; if not, interest is charged, and it is high.

So "debit" means money taken from what you have, and "credit" (on a card) means money borrowed that you pay back.

## Debit and credit in accounting

In bookkeeping (double-entry accounting), every transaction is recorded twice: as a **debit** in one account and a **credit** in another, for the same amount. Here the words are labels for the two sides of an entry, not "good" or "bad". For personal finance you rarely need this; the bank-statement meaning above is the one you see every day.

## Income and expense

- **Income:** money you earn or receive, such as salary, interest, dividends, rent received or a refund.
- **Expense:** money you spend, such as food, rent, bills, travel and shopping.
- **Savings** = income minus expenses for a period. If expenses are higher than income, you are spending savings or borrowing.

Moving money between your **own** accounts (bank to savings, paying your own credit card bill) is **neither** income nor expense: your total money did not change.

## Balance

Your **balance** is how much money is in an account at a moment. For a bank or savings account it is what you have. For a credit card, the outstanding balance is what you **owe**.

## Assets and liabilities

- **Assets:** what you **own** that has value: bank and savings balances, fixed deposits, mutual funds, stocks, gold, a house.
- **Liabilities:** what you **owe**: credit card dues, loans, EMIs still to be paid.
- **Net worth** = assets minus liabilities.

## Interest

**Interest** is the price of money:

- When you **save** or **lend** (a savings account, a fixed deposit), the bank pays you interest.
- When you **borrow** (a loan, an unpaid credit card bill), you pay interest.

**Simple interest** is calculated only on the original amount. **Compound interest** is also calculated on interest already earned, so it grows faster over time.

## Loans, principal and EMI

- **Principal:** the amount borrowed.
- **EMI (equated monthly instalment):** a fixed monthly payment that repays a loan. Each EMI has two parts: interest and principal. Early EMIs are mostly interest; later ones are mostly principal.
- **Tenure:** how long the loan runs.

## Cash flow

**Cash flow** is money in minus money out over a period, usually a month. Positive cash flow means you are saving; negative means you are spending more than you earn.
