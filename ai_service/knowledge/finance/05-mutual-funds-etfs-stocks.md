# Mutual funds, ETFs and stocks

## Stocks (shares)

A share is a small piece of ownership in a listed company. Its price moves every trading day with the company's results and with market mood.

- Possible gains: price growth and dividends.
- Risk: a single company can fall sharply or fail. Owning a few stocks concentrates risk.
- Buying and selling needs a demat and trading account with a SEBI-registered broker.

## Mutual funds

A mutual fund pools money from many investors. A professional fund manager invests it according to the fund's stated objective.

- **NAV (net asset value):** the price of one unit, calculated daily.
- **Diversification:** one fund holds many securities, so one bad company hurts less.
- **Expense ratio:** the yearly fee taken from the fund. A lower expense ratio leaves more return for you, and over many years the difference adds up.
- **Types:** equity (stocks, higher risk and higher long-term growth potential), debt (bonds, lower risk), hybrid (both), and **index funds** that simply copy an index such as the Nifty 50 at low cost.
- **Direct vs regular plans:** direct plans have no distributor commission and therefore a lower expense ratio.

## ETFs (exchange-traded funds)

An ETF is usually an index-tracking fund that is **traded on the stock exchange like a share**. Its price changes during the day.

- Usually very low cost.
- Needs a demat account.
- Can have low trading volume, so the price paid may differ slightly from the fund's actual value.

## SIP (systematic investment plan)

A SIP invests a fixed amount automatically every month in a mutual fund.

- It builds discipline: investing happens before spending.
- It buys more units when prices are low and fewer when high. This is called rupee cost averaging.
- It removes the need to "time the market".

## Risk and time horizon

| Time until you need the money | Usually suitable |
|---|---|
| Less than 1 to 3 years | Savings, FDs, low-risk debt funds |
| 3 to 5 years | Mix of debt and equity |
| More than 5 to 7 years | A larger share of equity can be considered, because time helps ride out falls |

Equity can fall 20 to 40% in bad years. Only invest money in equity that you will not need during such a fall.

## Warning signs

Be careful of anyone promising **guaranteed** high returns, tips on social media or messaging groups, or pressure to act immediately. SEBI regularly warns investors about such schemes. Check that advisers and brokers are registered with SEBI.

## In PPA

Record holdings as **assets** with the category Mutual Funds, Stocks or ETF. Update their values periodically, because PPA does not fetch live prices.

*This note is general education, not a recommendation to buy or sell any security.*
