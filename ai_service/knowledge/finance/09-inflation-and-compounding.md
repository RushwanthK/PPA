# Inflation and compounding

## Inflation

Inflation is the general rise in prices over time. In India, the Reserve Bank of India's policy aims to keep consumer price inflation around a target of 4%, within a band around it.

What it means for you: **₹100 today buys less in the future.** At 6% inflation, something costing ₹1,00,000 today costs about ₹1,79,000 in 10 years.

### Real return

The return that actually increases your buying power is:

**real return ≈ return − inflation**

A deposit earning 7% when inflation is 6% grows your buying power by only about 1% a year, and less after tax. Money kept for long periods only in a savings account slowly loses value.

## Compounding

Compounding means earning returns **on your earlier returns**, not just on the original amount.

Example: ₹1,00,000 growing at 10% a year:

| After | Value |
|---|---|
| 1 year | ₹1,10,000 |
| 10 years | about ₹2,59,000 |
| 20 years | about ₹6,73,000 |
| 30 years | about ₹17,45,000 |

The growth in the later years is much bigger than in the early years. That is why **starting early** matters more than starting with a large amount.

### Rule of 72

A quick estimate of how long money takes to double: **72 ÷ annual return (%) = years to double.**

- At 8%: about 9 years.
- At 12%: about 6 years.

The same rule shows how fast inflation halves the value of cash: at 6% inflation, buying power halves in about 12 years.

## Compounding works against you too

Debt compounds as well. An unpaid credit card balance at a high rate grows quickly, which is why clearing high-interest debt usually comes before investing.

*This note is general education, not personal financial advice. Example figures are illustrative and not a forecast of any investment's return.*
